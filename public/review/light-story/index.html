03 / KEEP THE SCIENCE INTACT

The ending is already in the experiment.

Prism → prepared green illumination

Rotation changes directions. A visible aperture selects one spectral band; spatial filtering and collimation prepare coherent light over both openings. A prism does not turn all colours into one.

Interference → individual arrivals

The camera moves into the same detector and the view changes to low-flux counting. Discrete absorbed-photon events build the predicted probability pattern.

A probability overlay can supply the final “fuzziness.” It represents where detections are likely, not particles travelling onward after absorption. The completed pattern remains the payoff.

Physics grounding: Feynman: probability amplitudes · Waterloo: single-photon pattern buildup.

04 / REUSE BEFORE REBUILDING

A practical source shortlist.

These projects can supply substantial parts of the scene. They still need art direction, accurate optical integration and a continuous camera sequence tailored to your content.

Poimandres / Prism

MIT

Reusable glass, beam, flare, rainbow and ray-hit components. The live demo was opened and interacted with, and the relevant source was inspected.

Adapt the physics: its demo multiplies a refraction angle by six. Keep our explicit two-face optics and substantially restrain its bloom.

Young's double slit / Nicolas Adamini

MIT

A finite-aperture WebGL wave-field shader with coherence and polarisation controls. The shader, wavelength conversion, model limits and licence were inspected.

It models classical wave optics. Photon detections need a separate probability-sampling layer. Shader cost still needs a phone benchmark.

MIT

Maintained scene helpers, transmission materials and scroll/camera patterns. The inspected Drei package targets React 19 and Fiber 9.

Use one scroll owner and one scene timeline. The homepage should keep ordinary document scrolling rather than trapping the visitor in nested scroll areas.

Other candidates and why they were not selected

Quantum Diffraction Simulator is MIT, but its inspected shader converts its nanometre wavelength value with 1e-6 instead of 1e-9. It also leaves mobile touch and 3D propagation on its future-work list. It needs correction and calibration before reuse as physics.

Nicky Case's double-slit declares CC0 for code and art. It is a helpful compact 2D teaching sketch; it does not supply the cinematic scene or a photon-counting model.

What Lumette contributes

Relay is the closest reference for the continuous prism-and-beam journey; Fieldmark is useful for photographic scale and depth. Their live experiences were inspected. They are commercial kits, not verified public open-source code. No purchase or kit download was made.

They guide the visual standard. Their listings do not establish tested performance on an actual iPhone in Safari.

THE NEXT PROOF

Earn the first three shots.

Before another full homepage build: a small working sequence from rotating glass to selected green illumination to two slits, with one large real photograph. Validate that on an actual iPhone before extending the same world to the completed pattern.

Visual acceptance

Clear, substantial glass; restrained light; seamless handoffs; prominent photos; readable personal content; no cartoon apparatus or controls over faces.

Interaction acceptance

Native scrolling, optional touch interaction, direct work links, reduced motion, a still fallback, and measured phone frame pacing and resource use.
